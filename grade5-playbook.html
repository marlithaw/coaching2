Section 01

Teacher's Tempo

The goal is not to win an argument. The goal is to make the routine more predictable than the chaos.

🎯

One Direction at a Time

Give a single direction. Let it land. Monitor. Do not stack instructions or explain during noncompliance.

🔇

Fewest Words Possible

Brief, calm, predictable language lowers ambiguity. Long explanations invite argument and raise threat in trauma-affected rooms.

🔁

Say It Once, Then Monitor

Repeat corrections invite negotiation. Say the direction once, scan the room, and follow through.

🚫

No Public Debates

Do not negotiate seat assignments. Do not lecture on profanity. Do not explain a move publicly. Correct quietly unless safety requires otherwise.

✅

Follow Through Every Time

Inconsistent follow-through teaches students the boundary is optional. If you say it, mean it — every single time.

🎙

Correct Privately When Possible

Public correction can become performance. Move to the student, use a low voice, and keep moving. Reserve whole-room resets for safety.

Economy of Language

Use this pattern for almost every correction: Name + Direction + Timeframe

Seat Correction
"Jordan, seat now."
Seat Correction
"Maya, back to your assigned seat. Now."
Language Correction
"Language. Try that again."
Attention Reset
"Eyes here in 3, 2, 1."
Why This Matters Here

In a trauma-affected classroom, long explanations often increase noise, invite argument, and raise perceived threat. Brief, calm, predictable language lowers ambiguity and makes the adult feel more stable. Students may not like the boundary — but they can learn it because it is consistent.

Section 02

What Students Will Be Taught

Teach these explicitly before expecting compliance. Students cannot follow a routine they were never taught.

Enter and go directly to your assigned seat.

  1. Walk into the room.
  2. Look at the board or posted seat chart.
  3. Go directly to your assigned seat.
  4. Sit down.
  5. Start the Do Now silently.
Teacher Script — Teaching This
"When you enter this room, there are five steps. Walk in. Find your assigned seat. Sit down. Start the Do Now. Stay silent unless I say otherwise. We are going to practice that until it is automatic."

Remain in your seat unless the teacher gives permission to move.

  1. Stay in your assigned seat during work time.
  2. Raise your hand or use the class signal if you need help.
  3. Keep working while waiting.
  4. Move only when the teacher gives permission.
Teacher Script — Teaching This
"During class, your seat is your workspace. If you need something, do not get up. Raise your hand or use the signal. I will come to you. You move when I give permission."
Script — On Future Movement
"Right now, assigned seats help us build a safe, focused classroom. As the class shows consistent success, I can reopen some movement privileges. Freedom grows with consistency."
Script — What Responsible Movement Looks Like
"Movement is part of a productive classroom, but it has to look responsible. Moving with purpose, staying on task, returning quickly, and not pulling other people off track."

Students Earn More Movement When They Consistently:

  • Enter and sit without redirection
  • Remain in assigned seats during independent work
  • Transition without roaming
  • Use appropriate language
  • Move with purpose and return quickly

Use school-appropriate language. No profanity.

  1. Pause before speaking.
  2. Use school-appropriate words.
  3. If profanity comes out, restate the message appropriately.
  4. If frustrated, use a replacement phrase.
Teacher Script — Teaching This
"In this room, frustration is allowed. Profanity is not. If the wrong word comes out, you will pause and say it again the school way."

Replacement Phrases — Post & Teach These

"I am frustrated." "Can you help me?" "I need a minute." "I do not like that." "Can I try again?"
Section 03

Arrival & First 10 Minutes

If students enter before the room is ready, the teacher starts from behind. The room should already be telling students what to do.

Before Students Arrive

Post the Do Now where students can see it from the doorway
Students should know what to do before they cross the threshold
Post or project the seating chart
Removes the "I don't know where to sit" excuse on entry
Put all needed materials on desks or in a visible pickup spot
Eliminates legitimate reasons to wander before class begins
Stand at the threshold before students enter
Physical presence at the door sets tone and allows individual direction on entry

At the Door

Door Script — Use This Every Time
"Good morning. Find your assigned seat. Do Now on the board."
Door Script — Add Time If Needed
"Good morning. Find your assigned seat. Do Now on the board. You have 3 minutes."
Do Not Say These
  • "Please sit down, everybody." — Too broad, too emotional, easy to ignore
  • "How many times do I have to tell y'all?" — Signals exasperation, invites argument
  • "Come on, guys, settle down." — No direction, no accountability

Once Students Begin Entering

For Students Who ARE Seated
Nod. Point to the paper. Quiet praise only: "That is it."
For Students Who Are NOT Seated
"Malik, assigned seat. Now." Point to the seat. Keep moving.
Launching the Do Now
"Eyes here in 3, 2, 1. You should be seated with your Do Now started."
Coaching Note

Do not wait for perfection to start. When most students are seated, begin. The teacher should not get trapped in one student at the expense of the room. Correct, move, and circle back.

Section 04

SOP — Assigned Seats

Assigned seats are not a punishment. They are a classroom regulation tool. They reduce social ignition, lower roaming, and help the teacher monitor quickly.

Introducing Assigned Seats
"I reset the room so we can learn faster and waste less time. Your assigned seat is part of that system. When you enter, you go to that seat every time unless I change it."

If a Student Challenges the Assignment

Use the least language possible. Pick one of these:

Option A
"That is your seat."
Option B
"New seat. Go there now."
Option C
"We are not debating seats. Go there now."
What Not to Do
  • Explain why the student was moved
  • Negotiate publicly
  • Let the student delay entry by arguing

If-Then Response Ladder — Seat Refusal

1
If
Student questions the seat once
Then
Restate briefly: "That is your seat. Go there now."
2
If
Student keeps talking but starts moving
Then
Let the movement count. Do not re-engage. Return to instruction.
3
If
Student refuses and remains standing
Then
Give a clear choice: "You may go to your seat now, or I will follow the next school step."
4
If
Student still refuses after the choice
Then
Implement the next campus consequence without argument — reset seat, buddy room, dean support, or office per school policy.
5
If
Student eventually complies at any step
Then
Do not add a lecture. Return directly to instruction.
Coaching Note

The danger is not the first complaint. The danger is allowing the complaint to become a performance for the room. The teacher should sound calm, not offended.

Section 05

SOP — Remaining Seated

This expectation must be concrete. Students need an exact definition — not a feeling.

What Counts as "Out of Seat"

  • Stand without permission
  • Walk to another student
  • Walk to the teacher without permission
  • Leave seat to show work or ask a question
  • Wander during transitions
Teaching Script
"When class begins, your seat is your workspace. You do not get up to sharpen a pencil, show me your screen, ask a question, or talk to a friend unless I give permission. I come to you."

Student-Friendly Version — Post This

  • The teacher says move
  • It is a planned transition
  • It is an emergency

If-Then Response Ladder — Out of Seat

1
If
Student stands or starts walking for a non-emergency reason
Then
Quick redirect: "Back to your seat." or "Seat. Now."
2
If
Student returns quickly
Then
Do not continue talking. Return to instruction immediately.
3
If
Student walks to the teacher to show work
Then
Do not reward the movement: "Return to your seat. I will come to you."
4
If
Student keeps moving or starts socializing while out of seat
Then
Use a choice: "You may go back to your seat now, or I will choose a different seat for you."
5
If
Student repeats behavior after correction
Then
Structured indoor recess, possible seat change, reflection sheet before returning to outdoor recess, teacher-student conference, or behavior referral per school policy.
6
If
Several students start moving at once
Then
Stop the class: "Freeze. Back to seats in 3, 2, 1." Once seated: "We are resetting. During work time, I move. You stay seated unless I give permission. Practice it now."
Coaching Note

Do not accidentally train students that standing up gets faster attention. If the teacher answers the student who walked up first, the teacher just taught the wrong system.

Section 06

SOP — Profanity

Profanity should be addressed because it damages safety and tone. It should not become a moral speech or a public showdown.

Teaching Script
"You are allowed to be upset. You are not allowed to use profanity in this room. If profanity comes out, you will pause and say it again the school way."

Three-Step Correction Sequence

1️⃣

Immediate Cue

Use the lightest correction first.
"Language."  |  "School words."  |  "Try that again appropriately."

2️⃣

Restatement

Student repeats the message without profanity. "This is stupid as hell." → "Try again with school words." → "This is frustrating."

3️⃣

Private Follow-Up

If student refuses to restate or repeats profanity, give a private consequence aligned to school policy.

If-Then Response Ladder — Profanity

1
If
Student blurts profanity out of frustration — not targeting a person
Then
Cue and restate: "Language. Try again."
2
If
Student restates appropriately
Then
Move on. No lecture. No follow-up in the moment.
3
If
Student uses profanity again after the cue
Then
Name the boundary: "That is profanity. You now owe me a reset conversation at the end of class."
4
If
Profanity is directed at another student or adult
Then
Treat as more serious immediately: "Stop. That language is not okay. Move to the reset seat now." Follow school policy for abusive or aggressive language.
5
If
Profanity spreads across multiple students
Then
Brief room reset: "Pause. Our room uses school-appropriate language. Frustration can stay. Profanity cannot. Reset your language and we continue."
What Not to Do
  • Argue about whether the student "meant it like that"
  • Start a long character lecture
  • Match the student's emotional intensity
  • Ignore repeated profanity once the expectation has been taught
Coaching Note

Profanity is often fast, impulsive, and emotional. The teacher's job is to make the response more regulated than the language that triggered it.

Section 07

Day 1 Script

Use this in order. Word for word if needed. The language is plain because pressure is real and the script has to hold under it.

At the Door Opening
"Good morning. Find your assigned seat. Start the Do Now on the board."
Once Most Students Are Seated Launch
"Eyes here in 3, 2, 1. Today we are resetting how this room works so learning can happen faster. I am teaching three expectations first. One, you go directly to your assigned seat when you enter. Two, you stay in your seat unless I give permission to move. Three, you use school-appropriate language. We are going to practice all three."
Teach — Assigned Seats Expectation 1
"When you walk in, you do not choose a seat. You go to your assigned seat. If you do not like the seat, that is still your seat. If I need to change it later, I will."
Teach — Remaining Seated Expectation 2
"During class, your seat is your workspace. Do not get up to ask me something, show me something, or talk to somebody. I will come to you."
Teach — Language Expectation 3
"You are allowed to be frustrated. You are not allowed to use profanity. If the wrong word comes out, I will stop you and you will say it again the school way."
Practice the Routine Drill
"We are going to practice entry. Everyone stand behind your chair. When I say go, you will step out, re-enter, find your assigned seat, sit down, and begin the Do Now silently."
"That was better. We will do it again until it is fast and quiet."
Section 08

Four-Day Rollout Plan

Click each day to expand actions and script. Build the habit across four days — less talking from the teacher each day, more automatic behavior from the class.

1
Day One
Teach
Teach all three expectations explicitly.
Teach the three expectations directly
Practice entry at least two times
Correct every out-of-seat behavior immediately
Teach profanity restatement language
End class with a brief review

"Eyes here in 3, 2, 1. Today we are resetting how this room works so learning can happen faster."

End of Class — Reflect On This

Who still resisted assigned seats? Who kept leaving their seat? Who needed profanity correction? Which correction script felt easiest to use?

2
Day Two
Tighten
Re-teach quickly. Emphasize speed and consistency.
Same door script as Day 1
One short reminder before the Do Now
Stronger follow-through on staying seated
Praise the routine, not personalities

"Today the goal is faster entry and fewer reminders. Show me you know the routine."

3
Day Three
Reduce
Tighten transitions. Reduce teacher talk.
Reduce reminders — let the room prove it
Monitor whether students begin independently
Catch repeated out-of-seat behavior early
Move profanity correction more privately

"By now, you know the system. I should not have to repeat it. Show it."

4
Day Four
Verify
Check whether the system holds with minimal prompting.
Run entry with almost no reminders
Track how many students begin correctly on their own
Notice who still needs individualized support

"Today I am watching to see what this class can do without extra reminders."

Section 10

Teacher Look-Fors

By the end of the first rollout cycle, these indicators should be improving. If they are, the system is working. If they are not, the teacher should not add more words — the teacher should tighten consistency.

More students entering and sitting without redirection
Less wandering during the first five minutes of class
Fewer students leaving seats during independent work time
Profanity corrected faster and with less classroom disruption
Less teacher talking during corrections — the economy of language is holding
If indicators are NOT improving: do not add more words. Tighten consistency.
The routine is the intervention.

When the structure holds, the teacher can teach. That is what this playbook is for.
